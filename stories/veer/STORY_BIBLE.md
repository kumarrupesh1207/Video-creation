## 1. Logline
In a neon-lit river city built on top of forgotten temples, nineteen-year-old **Veer** finds his own face carved
into a 5,000-year-old wall — and learns that every scripture on Earth is a memory humanity chose to forget.

**Central question [AGREED]:** *"What are we actually?"*
**Tagline [DECIDED-C]** (from the early cover): *"The Gods did not fall. We forgot them."*

## 2. Respect charter (non-negotiable) [AGREED + DECIDED-C]
1. Hindu Gods are shown as **saviours of a lost, highly advanced First Age** — protectors, never villains, never mocked. [AGREED]
2. Their power is **divine and scientific at once** — in the First Age dharma and science were one. The story never says "they were *only* technology". [DECIDED-C]
3. Gods appear mostly through **silhouettes, symbols, weapons and legend**, with reverence; they are never defeated, humiliated or sexualised. [DECIDED-C]
4. **No religion or faith is hurt or ranked.** Other cultures' gods are shown as **"echoes"** of the same lost memory, never as "X was really Y". [AGREED / DECIDED-C]
5. **No depiction of prophets** of any faith that forbids it; those traditions enter only through shared motifs (e.g., the great flood). [DECIDED-C]
6. Real scripture names (Vedas, Gita, Bible, Quran…) are **not** called "just memory files". The story's scriptures are **fictional texts inspired by** them (the *Smriti Sutras*). [DECIDED-C]
7. Opening card on every episode: *"A work of fiction inspired by the world's mythologies, told with respect."*

## 3. The world
### 3.1 The First Age — *Satya-Kalpa* [DECIDED-C]
- A global civilisation, tens of thousands of years ago, where knowledge (*Vidya*) and dharma were one discipline.
- Protected by the **Rakshaks** — the beings humanity later remembered as its Gods. They guarded, taught and healed; they did not rule.
- Its marvels survive as myth: **vimanas** (sky-vessels), **astras** (divine weapons), healing that looked like miracles, cities aligned to the stars.
- It was linked by the **Setu** — a planet-wide network of stone "memory nodes". Their ruins are the world's mysterious megaliths and temple foundations (Indian temples, Egyptian pyramids, Stonehenge-like circles, Maya pyramids). This is *how different times and lands are linked*. [AGREED: worldwide link · DECIDED-C: Setu]

### 3.2 The Fall and the Veil [AGREED premise · DECIDED-C mechanics]
- The First Age destroyed itself through greed and the weaponisation of Vidya.
- The survivors faced unbearable memory: every mind carried the horror of what they had done.
- Humanity **chose** to forget. **[AGREED]** The choice was made in the **Vismriti Yajna** — a ritual of collective consent.
- The forgetting is called **the Avaran** ("the Veil"). [proposal name kept]

**Veil rules (canon) [DECIDED-C]:**
1. The Veil seals **knowledge and memory, not objects**. Ruins, astras and Setu stones still exist — nobody remembers how they work.
2. The Veil can only be cast or broken by **the collective will of a civilisation**, enacted at the Setu. No single tyrant can impose it.
3. **Souls keep traces; minds forget.** [AGREED] Traces surface as déjà vu, dreams, triggers and impossible skills.
4. **Scriptures are leaks.** A few souls always remember fragments and write them as poetry and symbol — that is why every culture's sacred texts share motifs. [AGREED revelation]
5. The Veil has been cast **six times** — six civilisations rose, fell and forgot. We live in the seventh.
6. **If the Veil breaks suddenly**, people are flooded with all their past lives at once and lose their present identity ("**Smriti-flood**").

### 3.3 The present day — the Seventh Cycle
- Near-future Earth. The story starts in **Navkashi**, a fictional holy river megacity: ancient ghats below, neon towers above.
- The Veil is cracking. Smriti-floods have begun — first in Navkashi, later worldwide.
- The arc travels to echo-sites: **Egypt** (flood and judgement myths), **Greece** (sky-fire), **Scandinavia** (world tree and the end of days), **Mexico** (feathered serpent, cycles of suns), and Himalayan and ocean-floor ruins.

### 3.4 The Smritilaya [DECIDED-C]
The First Age's hidden archive under the Indian Ocean — every memory that was sealed, kept by the Rakshaks.
Whoever opens it decides how much of the past returns.

## 4. Characters
### Veer Pratap Rao — protagonist [AGREED core · DECIDED-C details]
- **19**, temple-restoration apprentice in Navkashi; quit an archaeology degree to raise his sister.
- **Want:** keep his little sister Meera safe and give her a normal life.
- **Need:** accept that he is more than one life — and choose who he wants to be.
- **Wound:** father (archaeologist Dr Aditya Rao) vanished on a dig 6 years ago; mother died soon after.
- **Triggers [AGREED]:** sacred symbols make him hear a war-drum in his chest; old scripts become readable for a second; his hands move with a warrior's training he never had.
- **Secret (major reveal, Arc 4):** in the First Age he was **Varun-Veer, a Rakshak-warrior who proposed the Veil**. He is responsible for humanity forgetting. [DECIDED-C]
- **Visual lock:** lean, tall, sharp jaw, messy black hair with one silver streak (grows with each recall), dark brown eyes that flash gold when triggered, rust-red scarf, dusty grey work kurta-jacket, a copper bracelet from his father.
- **Voice:** warm, quick, sarcastic under stress; English with a light Indian accent.

### Meera Rao — sister, 12 [DECIDED-C]
- Bright, stubborn, draws everything. **First Smriti-flood victim (Ch. 3):** wakes as a First-Age priestess who doesn't recognise Veer. Saving *her* present self drives Arc 1.

### Tara Iyer — linguist, 21 [DECIDED-C]
- Prodigy of dead scripts at Navkashi University; sceptic who wants proof. Decodes the Smriti Sutras.
- **Secondary romance [AGREED secondary]:** she remembers Veer from a past life before he remembers her — and is angry about it.

### Kabir Sheikh — best friend, 19 [DECIDED-C]
- Street-smart drone pilot and comic relief, loyal to the bone. **Dies at the end of Arc 3** protecting civilians during a city-wide Smriti-flood. ("Anyone can die" — AGREED.)

### Acharya Vyom — the mentor [DECIDED-C]
- Old keeper of the ruined Navkashi library. Secretly one of **the Seven Who Remember** (inspired by the Chiranjivi idea from the early game concept, kept as an *echo*, with fictional names).

### The Seven Who Remember [DECIDED-C]
Immortal witnesses who kept memory through every cycle, scattered worldwide. Each guards one Setu site and one fragment.
Introduced one or two per arc. Respectful, wise, never comic.

### SMARAN, the Unforgotten — main antagonist [AGREED motive · DECIDED-C details]
- A Rakshak of the First Age and the **Eighth** who remembered — Varun-Veer's sworn brother.
- He voted *against* the Veil and was condemned to remember all six falls.
- **Motive [AGREED]:** forgetting is humanity's escape from consequence. This time everyone will face the truth — learn or end.
- He is not evil; he is grief with a plan. He wants to **shatter the Veil at once**, knowing the Smriti-flood will kill millions.
- **Visual:** tall, scarred bronze armour fused with stone, hair of ash-white flame, eyes like dying stars.

### The Three Heralds — Smaran's lieutenants (multiple villains [AGREED])
- **Jalaghna, Herald of the Flood** (Arc 1–2) — controls water and drowning memories. Echo: flood myths.
- **Agnira, Herald of Fire** (Arc 2–3) — burns the present to reveal the past. Echo: sky-fire and end-of-world myths.
- **Taraka, Herald of the Stars** (Arc 3–4) — reads every past life and turns people against themselves. Echo: star calendars and cycles of suns.
Each believes in Smaran for a personal reason; at least one turns.

## 5. Power system — *Smriti* (memory is power) [DECIDED-C]
- Powers come from **recalling a past life**. Each recall grants an **Ansh** (fragment) — a skill, weapon or astra from that life.
- **The cost (Vinimay — "exchange"):** every recall **erases one memory of the present life** (a birthday, a friend's voice, Meera's laugh). Power and identity trade against each other — that is the theme.
- **Tiers** (mid-level start → god-level [AGREED trajectory]):
  1. **Jagrit** (Awakened) — triggers, reflexes. *(Veer at Ch. 1)*
  2. **Smrit** (Recalled) — one past-life skill at will.
  3. **Purva** (Ancestral) — summon a past-life weapon (Veer: the **light-trident astra**, from the early cover art).
  4. **Deva-Ansh** (Divine fragment) — First-Age power; fights Heralds.
  5. **Param** (Supreme) — full Rakshak power; at this level he risks forgetting his present self entirely.
- Astras are *remembered*, not found — they form from light when the memory is complete.

## 7. Ending — the Third Path [DECIDED-C, hopeful with cost]
- Smaran wants to **shatter** the Veil (truth with no mercy). The old way is to **re-seal** it (mercy with no truth).
- Veer's answer: open the Smritilaya so humanity keeps **the lessons, not the pain** — every person receives their past lives as **story**, not as flood. "We wrote myths so we could remember without breaking."
- **Cost:** to carry the full memory through the Setu, Veer spends his own past lives — he becomes *only* Veer again, and loses his First-Age self and powers. Smaran dies in his arms, at peace, seeing humanity choose to remember gently.
- Kabir's death and the destruction stay real. The ending is hopeful, not reset.
- **The prophesied saviour of the dark age** (the early "Kali Avatar" idea): the story **never claims** Veer is that saviour and never claims the prophecy is fulfilled. It is left as a revered future promise. [DECIDED-C, out of respect]

## 9. Style guide for the motion comic
- **Manga look:** cel-shaded, strong ink lines, screentone shading, dramatic speed lines on action panels; colour palette of saffron, river-teal, night-indigo and ember-red.
- **Dialogue first:** characters speak their own lines in their own voices; narration only for time jumps and scripture verses (verses in a parchment box, read by a calm narrator).
- **Scripture panels:** glowing script that briefly turns readable when Veer is triggered — the signature visual.
- **Respect:** deities shown as silhouettes or ancient reliefs, never as fighters in panels.
